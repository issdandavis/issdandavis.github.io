## Final narration

How many ways can one byte tell the truth?

Binary. Negabinary. Balanced ternary. Six symbolic languages. A color. A tone.
A coordinate in curved space.

Each view can reveal something different. But none of them is allowed to forget
where it came from.

Start with the physical carrier. Eight bits give one byte exactly two hundred
and fifty-six possible identities. Rewriting that byte in another radix may
expose sign, symmetry, phase, adjacency, or a convenient route. The
representation changes. The source identity does not.

That is the central constraint in *Trits, Bits, and Bobs*: one exact object,
many operational views, and one route home.

The saved shared-byte audit contains two hundred and fifty-six synchronized
bundles. Its coordinate charts and tongue mappings round-trip to the original
byte. That is measured evidence for identity and reversible transport. It is
not evidence that the views create additional information.

Now count the state honestly.

A paired six-state cell combines one binary sign axis with one three-level
magnitude axis. Two times three gives six addresses: negative and positive
zero, one, and two. The two zeros share a numerical magnitude, but the schema
can preserve different orientation metadata.

A dual-trit field is a different object. One trit records region polarity. A
second records local orientation or evidence. Three times three gives nine hard
addresses only when both axes remain independently meaningful.

Draw a mirror pair, t and negative t, on the same three-by-three board and only
three positions are reachable. A large ambient grid is not proof of a large
independent state space.

The same discipline applies when a representation reaches a model.

Logging a trit, a color, or a geometric coordinate for a human does not teach a
neural network. A view must actually enter the forward computation, the loss,
memory, or an action policy through an explicit interface. Then it must survive
comparison with an unchanged parent, a size-matched control, shuffled bindings,
and repeated seeds.

A diagram is not an integration. A tracked variable is not yet a learned
capability.

This is why every technical scene carries an evidence badge.

Implemented means executable source exists. Measured means a named run left a
receipt. Proposed means the mechanism still needs a controlled experiment.

The byte, radix, and Six Tongues transport machinery is implemented, and its
finite round trips are measured. Broader claims that trit, geometric, or
multiview inputs improve learned capability remain proposals until they beat
the right controls.

One last boundary: reversible is not the same as secret. A tongue token, radix
transform, or phase label is not encryption. Confidentiality still requires
standard cryptographic primitives and keys.

The strength of the architecture is not that more names create more
information. It is that one exact object can participate in many useful
operations without losing its origin—and that every transform is asked for its
inverse, its test, its receipt, and its limitation.

If you want to follow one byte as it becomes trits, tongues, geometry, model
inputs, routes, and governed action, the rabbit hole is open.

Bring a pencil. The receipts are part of the story.
