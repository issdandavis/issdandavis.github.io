Writing

Keep the story.
Let the model lay track.

I coach people who want to write with AI and still recognize the book at the end. You pick the plot. You write the sentences. The model is allowed to use your structure and your voice to join those sentences. It does not get to invent the next town.

One sentence at a time A long draft is a sequence of jobs. Name the job, then write the sentence that does it.
Your words stay yours I will not hand you a new scene and call it a polish. If a line was not yours, it does not go in.
Stop when it swells Speed spends the time you needed to process the work. Stepping back is part of writing. Other writers do it with an editor or an agent. You can do it too.
The pass

What we do with a draft.

This is the method I used on my own essay, sentence by sentence. Eighty-four sentences, each one kept, each one given a job. The notes name what a phrase is doing. They are not a replacement paragraph.

1

You choose the plot

The destination is yours before any model opens. A chapter, a scene, or one claim. If you do not know the next town, we stop and pick it. We do not ask the model to pick it for you.

2

Name the sentence

Open the subject. Paint the picture. Narrow it. Answer what someone else would say. State the claim. Give the example. Say why it matters. Close. A sentence can do more than one of these. The label does not prove the job got done.

3

Length is a choice

Past about thirty words, we look for a split. Under about ten words, the short line stays only if you meant the punch. Going back through a finished book is the same move. That return is the work, not a failed draft.

4

Then the railway

Only after the sentences exist may a model couple them. It may use your order and your voice. It may not add a motive, a fact, or an ending you did not write. If the join changes the story, the join comes out.
