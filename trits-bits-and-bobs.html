The small piece of candy

Many views. One origin.

Binary, balanced ternary, negabinary, a tongue token, and a geometric coordinate can expose different structure while still pointing back to the same byte.

Invariant
“A glyph is an address, not a proof.”

The book keeps the source identity underneath every abstraction. A new view may make a route easier to see; deterministic re-encoding does not create new independent entropy.

A two-minute state laboratory

Six is not nine.

The interaction rule determines the state space. Pair one sign bit with one magnitude trit and you get six states. Give two trits independent jobs and you get nine.

Exact state

Paired six-state cell

2 × 3 = 6

A sign bit selects positive or negative. A magnitude trit selects zero, one, or two. Signed zero remains addressable.

Use this when the two fields have different jobs and the allowed combinations are exactly six.

Claims carry labels

The book tells you what is solid—and what is not.

A mechanism, a measurement, and a hypothesis are not allowed to borrow one another’s certainty.

Implemented

Exact transport

The Six Tongues execute reversible byte mappings. The strongest current claim is transport, typed routing, and consistency checking—not encryption.

Measured

Replayable surfaces

Finite state tables, round-trip tests, generated diagrams, source hashes, and build receipts give the reader something concrete to inspect.

Proposed

Learning benefits

Claims that multiview inputs improve models still require direct-input tests, size-matched controls, shuffled bindings, and repeated seeds.
